Publish a field guide to the forty commonest birds of the Hollin Valley that a first-time walker can use to name a bird they are looking at, without a phone signal.

THE FINISH LINE is the printer's signed proof of the complete guide, held by the print shop and not by this project. A draft the project says is finished is not finished; the signed proof is.

The reader is a walker who has never used a field guide, not the chairman who commissioned it. An entry that needs a term the reader has not been taught is wrong for them, however correct it is.

This is judged against what a published regional field guide contains: a plate, a range note, a season note and the confusion species for every bird. The list of forty birds below is what the chairman knew to ask for; the brief is a subset of the standard, not the whole of it. Where such a guide covers something he did not name, it is in scope.

Pages drafted, birds researched and words written are not progress, and this project is not graded on them. Only entries that pass the naming test count.

Every entry is tested the same way before it counts: a person who has not seen the entry is shown three photographs and must name the bird from the entry alone. Record who was tested and what they answered.

A bias, not a rule: prefer drawings to photographs for the plates, because drawings show the marks that matter. If a photograph identifies a bird better, use it and say why.

The quality bar is that no entry sends a reader to the wrong bird, because a guide that is wrong once is not trusted again. The editor owns that bar and may hold any entry back.

The chairman will answer questions about the valley and approve the final proof. He will not write entries, choose plates or check facts; those are the project's.

Spend carefully until the first ten entries pass the naming test, because the method is unproven until then. Once they pass, spend what the remaining thirty need.

If the proof cannot be signed, what is owed instead is every entry that passed its naming test, bound as a shorter guide, with a list of the birds left out and why.
